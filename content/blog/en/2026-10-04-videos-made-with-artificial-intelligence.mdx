I owe you an explanation. My work schedule no longer allows me to record the course videos the way I used to: hours at the sequencer, writing every example, recording my voice, editing, and starting over whenever something went wrong. If I kept going like that, each lesson would take months, and that isn't fair to the people studying with me.

So I made a decision: starting with [Lesson 5](/en/harmony-course/06-lesson-5-degrees-chords-tonality), the videos are made with the help of artificial intelligence. I apologize if you miss seeing me at the sequencer, but I also want to tell you why I'm so excited.

## How a video is made now

- **The content is the same as always.** Everything comes from the course I took with my teacher, Medrano, and have taught for years. The AI doesn't invent the theory: it works from my notes and checks itself against them.
- **The script** is prepared by an AI assistant from the lesson, sentence by sentence, and I correct it. In Lesson 5, for example, I asked it to use the Spanish terms "oncena" and "trecena", and to call the period of Schubert, Chopin and Brahms "classical and romantic".
- **The voice is a clone of mine**, created with ElevenLabs from my recordings. It sounds like me because it is made from my voice, but I want to be clear: I didn't record it sentence by sentence.
- **The scores and graphics** come from our own sequencer and from the Virtual Teacher's engine, so every note you see is checked by the same code that checks your assignments.
- **The musical examples** are chosen and mixed by me.
- **At the end**, I watch the whole video, ask for corrections, edit in my intro and outro, and publish it.

Several AI assistants work as a team: one writes the script and checks the music, another builds the tools and assembles the video, and everything goes through automated tests before it reaches you.

## Completing our dreams

I've carried this course in my head since 1988. I wrote it down in pencil and pen, taught it in class, and recorded videos whenever I could. I always dreamed of having it complete, in order, in two languages, with a tool that checks students' exercises. For years I thought that would take more time than I have.

Today Lesson 5 has its video in English and Spanish, in my voice, with graphics I could never have drawn on my own and a musical example from every stage of the history of harmony. It was made in a single day.

That is what moves me most about this moment: artificial intelligence doesn't replace what we know or what we love; it gives us back the time to finish what we started. I believe that's true for everyone: the musician who always wanted to publish their method, the teacher who wants to reach more students, anyone with a project kept in a drawer waiting for "when I have time".
