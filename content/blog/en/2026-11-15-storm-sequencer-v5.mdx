You can now open [Storm Sequencer v5](/en/sequencer/v5) from the [harmony course page](/en/harmony-course). This version brings notation, tracks and writing tools together on one screen. Work on a melody or all four SATB voices, listen to your music and export a MIDI exercise to submit to the Virtual Teacher.

One new way to experiment is microphone input: **sing a note and see it written in the score**. Since a single human voice cannot comfortably cover soprano, alto, tenor and bass, there is also a selector for choosing the octave in which your singing is written.

## Sing comfortably and choose the written register

Beside **Mic**, **Write at** lets you choose the original pitch, one octave up or down, or two octaves up or down.

For example, sing **C3** with **1 octave up (+12)** selected and the score receives **C4**. The display shows both the heard note and the note to be written. You choose the shift; notes already in the score stay as they are.

To try it:

1. Stop playback, choose a voice and place the cursor at the measure where you want to begin.
2. Pick a note value, such as a quarter note, and choose an octave under **Write at**.
3. Press **Mic** and allow microphone access.
4. Sing one sustained note at a time, leaving a short silence between notes.
5. Switch **Mic** off and press play to hear the result.

Microphone entry writes pitches using the note value you selected: **it does not save a recording of your voice or automatically transcribe your rhythm**. Input pauses during score playback so the microphone does not write what comes from your speakers. Microphone audio is processed on your computer.

## A studio for writing and listening

You can also write with the mouse, computer keyboard, musical text or a compatible MIDI keyboard. The studio includes continuous and page notation views, a piano roll, multiple-note selection, copy and paste, per-voice instruments and volume, chord symbols and scene markers.

Save your project and export MIDI, MusicXML, WAV audio or score images. The sequencer gives you space to think and write: exercise review remains a separate step when you submit your work to the Virtual Teacher.
