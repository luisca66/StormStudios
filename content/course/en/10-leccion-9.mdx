## How a phrase ends

You can already build chords in the vocal quartet and connect them. Now you will learn how to **end a phrase**: a cadence is a succession of chords with a conclusive intention, moving toward more and more important tonal regions, from the subdominant to the dominant and the tonic.

In the video you will see the **four types of cadence**: **authentic** (V–I), **plagal** (IV–I), **complete** (IV, II or VI – V – I) and **extended** (IV or II – I6/4 – V – I), with their common rule: the last two chords are in root position. You will also meet the first use of **I6/4**, always on a strong beat, and **half cadences**, the partial rests of the chorale.

## Assignment for the Virtual Teacher

In Storm Sequencer, in SATB mode and 4/4 time, write in **a single file** **one cadence of each type**: authentic, plagal, complete and extended, in any order.

1. Write each cadence in a **different major key** and put its **key signature** at the beginning.
2. Each cadence starts in a **new measure**; every chord is a **half note** (the final I may be a whole note). That is **11 chords**.
3. Chords without a figure are in root position.

Export the MIDI and upload it: the Virtual Teacher recognizes the key and type of each cadence and checks the chords, the inversions, the I6/4 on a strong beat, the voice leading, parallels and the leading tone. It does not check anything between one cadence and the next.
