## Building chords on a scale

A fifth chord, or **triad**, is built by stacking two thirds. Its notes are the **root**, the **third** and the **fifth**. Starting on C, for example, we get C–E–G.

To build the chords of a scale, take each degree as the root and stack thirds **using the notes and accidentals of that scale**.

## The four types of fifth chords

| Type | Third above the root | Fifth above the root |
|---|---|---|
| Major | Major | Perfect |
| Minor | Minor | Perfect |
| Diminished | Minor | Diminished |
| Augmented | Major | Augmented |

## Scales and their chords

In the video, we build and listen to the chords of **C natural and harmonic major**, and **C natural, harmonic and melodic minor**.

In C natural major, the chords on degrees I, IV and V are major; those on II, III and VI are minor; the chord on VII is diminished.

Harmonic major lowers the sixth degree. For the minor scales, we use the **C minor key signature: B♭, E♭ and A♭**. Harmonic minor raises the seventh degree to B♮; ascending melodic minor also raises the sixth to A♮. Descending melodic minor returns to the notes of natural minor.

These accidentals change the intervals and therefore the types of chords built on each degree. Always check the third and fifth above the root.
