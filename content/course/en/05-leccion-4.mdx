## Fifth chords

A fifth chord, or **triad**, is built by stacking two thirds: **root**, **third** and **fifth**. When we use each degree of a scale as a root, only four kinds of chords appear: **major**, **minor**, **diminished** and **augmented**.

In the video we build and listen to the chords of C major natural and harmonic, and of C minor natural, harmonic and melodic, and see how each accidental changes the chord type on each degree.

## Your assignment

Build the chords on every degree of all the basic scales and submit them in **a single MIDI file**:

- Tonics: **C, C♯, D, E♭, E, F, F♯, G, A♭, A, B♭ and B**.
- For each tonic: natural major, harmonic major, natural minor, harmonic minor, and melodic minor ascending and descending.
- Each series goes **I–II–III–IV–V–VI–VII–I′** (descending melodic minor goes from I′ down to I; you may omit its opening tonic).
- Three-note simultaneous triads, in root position and close position, without inversions.

The series can come in any order: the Virtual Teacher recognizes them, tells you which are missing and explains any errors. In Storm Sequencer use **a single staff** and hold **Ctrl** while you **click** to add the third and the fifth.
