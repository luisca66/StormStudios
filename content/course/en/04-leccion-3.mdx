## The minor scales

Every major scale has a **relative minor** that shares its key signature but centers on a different **tonic**: the 6th degree of the major. C major and A minor, for example, use exactly the same notes.

In the video you will meet the **three forms of the minor scale** (natural, harmonic and melodic), hear how each one sounds, and see why the melodic minor comes down differently from the way it goes up.

## Exercise

Play the **15 minor keys** in this order:

- Sharps: A · E · B · F♯ · C♯ · G♯ · D♯ · A♯ minor
- Flats: D · G · C · F · B♭ · E♭ · A♭ minor

For each key, on a single channel (Soprano): **natural** minor ascending (8 notes), **harmonic** minor ascending (8), **melodic** minor ascending (8) and **melodic** minor descending (7). That is **31 notes per key**.

Watch the spelling: each degree uses its own letter (in A harmonic minor the 7th degree is **G♯**, not A♭). Export the MIDI file and upload it to the Virtual Teacher.
