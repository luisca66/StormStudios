Now let's go back to fifth chords.
If we take each degree of the basic scales as a root, we only get four types of chords: major, minor, diminished and augmented.
A chord is major or minor because of its third, and augmented or diminished because of its fifth.
The chords of a scale are grouped into three tonal regions: the subdominant region, the tonic region and the dominant region.
Each region has a principal chord that gives it its name, and two secondary chords, a third above and a third below it.
If we arrange the chords of C major in thirds, it becomes very clear.
The subdominant region has the fourth degree as its principal chord, and the second and sixth as secondary chords.
The tonic region has the first degree, with the sixth and the third.
And the dominant region has the fifth degree, with the third and the seventh.
Notice that the third and the sixth, the modal chords, belong to two regions at the same time.
Chords of the same region can substitute for each other without changing the harmonic sense of a passage.
Finally, let's talk about tonality.
We call tonality the set of relationships, functions and hierarchies that all the sounds of our musical system adopt around one of them, which we choose as the tonal center and call the tonic.
To modulate is to replace one tonal center with another.
We will study it later.
The altered, or unaltered, notes of a passage tell us its key.
If we order the keys by the order in which new accidentals appear, we discover a fixed relationship of perfect fifths.
Sharps appear in this order: F, C, G, D, A, E and B.
Each one is a perfect fifth above the previous one.
Flats appear in the opposite order: B, E, A, D, G, C and F.
Each one is a perfect fifth below the previous one.
With this we build the circle of fifths.
At the top is C major, with no accidentals.
Going clockwise, each key is a perfect fifth above the previous one and adds a sharp: G, D, A, E, B, F sharp and C sharp.
Going counterclockwise, each key is a perfect fifth below and adds a flat: F, B flat, E flat, A flat, D flat, G flat and C flat.
At the bottom of the circle, three positions have two names.
B and C flat, F sharp and G flat, C sharp and D flat sound the same, but they are written with different key signatures.
In total we have fifteen major keys.
Each key signature also serves a minor key, its relative, a minor third below.
A minor is the relative of C major.
E minor, of G major.
